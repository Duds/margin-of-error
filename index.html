Margin of Error

The gap between what polling said and what happened

Across every federal election since 1996, did polling overstate or understate One Nation's vote, and does the published margin of error explain the difference?

This is an open test, not a verdict. The charts publish whichever way the numbers fall, including the possibility that polling was accurate. Every figure carries a primary-source citation, and every poll-derived number carries the margin of error its publisher stated, or a visible marker saying no margin was published.

What this page measures

The question is narrow on purpose. It covers the party's primary vote in House of Representatives elections since 1996, using only pollster published tables. Senate results, party capability and volunteer coverage are excluded, because none of them can be verified to the standard this page holds itself to.

This figure is a working draft and its numbers are not real. Every figure on the finished page will resolve to a document in the project's source registry that a reader can open.
